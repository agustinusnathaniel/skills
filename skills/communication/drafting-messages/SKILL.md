# Drafting Messages

A draft posted under someone's name spends their credibility. Write only what
the author can defend, check it, and hand over a final text that is never in
doubt.

## Write what the author can defend

- Every claim matches live data or the current source; drop what is
  unverified, or make it a question.
- Every number says what it measured, how, and over which window.
- Every cited source and every PR or issue reference is a full link.
- State the plain mechanism instead of internal shorthand or labels.
- Explain a finding new to the author before it goes in their draft.
- Commit them only to dates, estimates, and work they have stated.

## Edit their draft in place

Keep every point they made; cut only unsourced claims and unasked lecturing,
fix facts and links, and say what changed. Across rounds, check each version
against everything they asked for.

## Check, then hand over

1. For a draft with factual claims or one going outside the team, run a
   separate checker pass (a fresh subagent where available) against the
   sources, the destination, and the author's earlier asks; a quick
   self-check is enough otherwise.
2. Hand over a short note of what was checked, then the full final text as
   the last thing in the message. When the author asks for the final version
   or the draft changed, print it whole, not a list of edits.
3. Post or save only the exact approved text, through a channel you are
   authorized to use, then read the rendered post back.
